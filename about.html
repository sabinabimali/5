About

I am a Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering at Purdue University, where my research focuses on reliability assessment and metrology of thermal interface materials (TIMs). I develop experimental approaches to understand how thermal greases degrade under thermal cycling and dynamic surface warpage, with the broader goal of improving thermal management in electronics packaging.

My work combines experimental testing, material characterization, and thermal modeling. I am also interested in how rheological properties relate to TIM performance, and how package-level design choices influence temperature distribution and thermal resistance.

Previously, I worked on sustainable additively printed electronics at Auburn University and as a Mechanical Design Engineer in Nepal. In summer 2026, I joined Qualcomm in San Diego as a Thermal Intern, where I benchmarked commercial mobile devices and modeled electronic packages with and without heat-spreader lids.

I enjoy interdisciplinary engineering, hands-on experimentation, and translating research findings into practical guidance for reliable electronic systems. I am interested in thermal engineering opportunities and collaborations.

Research interests

Thermal interface material degradation under dynamic warpage

Designing a test structure to evaluate thermal grease degradation under controlled thermally and mechanically induced warpage. The work includes warpage measurement, thermal cycling, and studying the relationship between degradation and material rheology.

Thermal behavior of electronic packages

Analytical and numerical modeling of packages with and without heat-spreader lids, comparing temperature distributions, thermal resistance, and operating conditions where lidless configurations may outperform lidded designs.

Sustainable printed electronics and thermal isolation

Prior research includes flexible hybrid printed electronics using water-based inks, biodegradable substrates, and low-temperature processing, as well as mechanical characterization of CMOS-compatible aerogel materials for thermal isolation.
